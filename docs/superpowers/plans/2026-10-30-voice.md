# Voice: faster, louder but not too loud, a voice most people like (research and plan, 30 Oct 2026)

**Owner's words:** speed the voice up; louder but not that much louder; use a voice that men, women and teenagers all love to hear; research first, load skills before implementing.

## Research (labels: REPORTED = third party only, MEASURED = by me today)
- **Licence risk (REPORTED, a Microsoft Q&A community answer and vendor blogs; Microsoft's own terms not read):** the studio's narrator is edge-tts, the unofficial route to the service behind Edge's Read Aloud. Commercial use without an Azure subscription could break Microsoft's terms, there is no guarantee it keeps working, and it has already been restricted in places. Every monetized video's voice rests on it. Options: (A) keep it (free, risk), (B) Azure AI Speech: same voice names, official, has HD versions of Andrew, Ava, Brian, Emma (needs an account and a key the owner enters in Settings; the SDK gives the word timings the captions need; pricing and the free tier to be read from Microsoft's page before any choice), (C) Kokoro-82M: Apache-2.0 weights (VERIFIED on its Hugging Face card, code licence not stated there), 54 voices, runs locally, speed on CPU not measured, needs a new dependency and the owner's yes.
- **Pace (REPORTED, vendor blogs, no study of retention or of teenagers):** documentary narration is usually 130 to 150 words a minute; above about 160 it reads as an explainer; a vendor sample of 53 long voiceovers had a median of 166.
- **Loudness (REPORTED, audio vendors, not an official YouTube number):** about -14 LUFS integrated with a true peak of -1 to -1.5 dB. The studio's own note says YouTube turns louder uploads down and never turns quiet ones up, so louder than -14 has no benefit.

## Measured today
- Today's voice (Andrew, -6%, -3 Hz) speaks at 158 words a minute while talking; the whole film is 143 because of the pauses (0.6 s a line).
- The voices differ a lot at the same setting: at +0% Andrew 168, Andrew-Multilingual 155, Ava-Multilingual 156, Aria 172, Christopher 172, Brian-Multilingual 173, Emma-Multilingual 175, Jenny 175, Guy 186 words a minute. A speed change must be set per voice (`rate = target / base - 1`).
- Today's samples sit at -16.8 LUFS. The studio asks for -14 and gets about -15 because `loudnorm` runs in one pass. A plain two-pass run on the raw voice (-21.1 LUFS) reaches -14.8; with a 3:1 compressor first it reaches the target exactly (-14.5 and -14.0 both hit) at a -1.5 dB true peak, and the loudness range falls from 2.4 to 1.6 to 1.8 LU (steadier, easier on phone speakers). After the lossy mp3 step the listening files measure -14.9 to -15.0.
- Nine voices at the same pace (169 to 171 words a minute) and the same loudness are on a private page: https://claude.ai/artifact/1ZwX7CqZF1ykYJQ3KAMhn4. A voice's appeal to men, women and teenagers cannot be measured by me; only listening tests can say, and no source I found measured it.

## Plan (after the owner listens and chooses)
1. Owner: pick one or two voices; say yes or no to loudness -14.5 with the compressor; pick the engine path (A, B or C above).
2. Voice settings: default voice and a per-voice base speed in `config.py` and `tts.py`; `tts.rate_for(voice, target_wpm)`; target pace about 170 words a minute while speaking (about 155 for the whole film).
3. Audio finish: compressor then two-pass `loudnorm` on the narration (I -14.5, TP -1.5), tests against a real ffmpeg measurement, an `agenteval` case with mutation checks, reviewer range unchanged.
4. If B or C: a new engine module behind the same interface (word timings in, mp3 out), dependency scan, owner's yes.
Nothing here is applied to the studio yet.
