## 5. The choice
Candidates scored on: proof of demand now, a documented record (no invented facts), pictures possible without realistic people, fit with the channel's "calm, sourced" promise, no clash with Autopilot's queue.
| Story | Demand | Record | No people needed | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flannan Isles lighthouse keepers (1900)** | live 1,506 views at 13 subs; a 652 k Short this week | National Records of Scotland page and papers, Wikipedia, Skeptoid | lighthouse, sea, ship | **chosen** |
| Roman dodecahedron | 1,451 views at 242 subs | no agreed purpose; the web is full of "solved" clickbait | an object (Blender can model it) | second choice; hard to stay honest |
| Mary Celeste | 1,395 views | good | ship | Autopilot already queued it on 31 Oct: skip to avoid a clash |
| Roanoke | 2,176 views at 41 subs | good | forest, coast | heavily covered |
Angle (ours alone): **the record against the legend.** The NRS record is short: three keepers, a dark light on 15 Dec, the Hesperus on 26 Dec, an empty station, a last log entry of 13 Dec, storm damage at the west landing, an inquiry that suggested a large wave. The famous details (praying keepers in the log) are fiction according to the researcher Mike Dash; Skeptoid dates their first print to 1965. Mainstream articles (for example History.co.uk) still repeat the toppled chair and the uneaten meal, which Skeptoid traces to a 1912 poem. That difference between sources is left out of the Short (see Limits) but is the spine of a later long film.

## 6. Limits
- 15 days of data, 10 subscribers: nothing here is statistically firm.
- Competitor numbers are public view counts at one moment; I did not watch the videos, only read their titles, lengths, dates and counts.
- Click-through, impressions and unique viewers are unknown.
- Sources differ on small details; the Short uses only what two sources support.
