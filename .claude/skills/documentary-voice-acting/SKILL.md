# Documentary voice acting

The narrator is a trusted guide, not an actor in the scene. Emotion is real but held back; the facts carry the weight.

## By kind of passage
- **History, explanation**: warm, even, moderate pace; let dates and names land with a small pause after them.
- **Mystery**: curious, slightly lower and slower before the key detail; a short pause before the reveal, not after every sentence.
- **Suspense**: controlled, quieter, deliberate pace; build across two or three lines, then release. No whisper for a whole chapter.
- **Tragedy, loss**: slower, softer, serious; no smile in the voice, no vocal events; a longer pause after the hardest sentence.
- **Discovery, surprise**: a lift in energy on the discovered fact only, then back to calm.
- **Legend, hoax, absurd invention**: lightly amused at most; a chuckle only if the text itself is absurd and the model supports it.
- **Endings**: reflective, slower, sincere; the last question asked plainly, not dramatically.

## Common faults
- One emotion for the whole video (monotone) or a new emotion on every line (melodrama).
- Rising pitch on statements; drama on ordinary facts; laughter near a death.
- Rushing names and numbers; no breath between chapters.

## Checking a sample
Listen to one minute from the opening, one from the saddest passage and the ending. Each should differ, and none should sound like a different person.
