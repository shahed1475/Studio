## Why the Mary Celeste
- Central question: why did ten people leave a brigantine that could still sail, with its cargo and supplies on board (found 4 Dec 1872, east of the Azores)?
- Evidence: the salvage inquiry at the Vice-Admiralty Court in Gibraltar (1873) and later research, all summarised with references in the Wikipedia article the studio reads as its source.
- A natural myth-versus-record arc: Arthur Conan Doyle's 1884 story "J. Habakuk Jephson's Statement" spread invented details (warm tea, a half-eaten meal) that many retellings still repeat. The film can separate the record from the fiction, which suits the channel's facts-first rules.
- Visuals: the ship and its captain (period images on Commons), the Dei Gratia, Gibraltar, the Azores, sea charts, 19th-century shipping.
- The Short: one clear idea ("a ship found sailing with no one on board") and one twist (the famous details came from a story by the author of Sherlock Holmes).

## Limits
- Readership is Wikipedia reading, not YouTube search volume; no Google Trends figure was used (none could be read lawfully by a script).
- The topic is evergreen, not trending (1.03x).
